💡 Pro Tip

Mix minor and major pentatonic in the same solo for maximum blues expression. Start with minor, then "borrow" notes from major for colorful moments. This is what B.B. King did!

Essential Techniques

These techniques give blues its voice. Master each one slowly, then combine them for expressive playing.

↗️

String Bends

Push a string sideways to raise its pitch. The most expressive blues technique.

Full Bend Half Bend Quarter Bend
Full bend example (A minor pent): e|---8---10pb(10)---8---| B|----------------------| pb = push bend to next note

🎯 Tip: Use 2 fingers for strength. Bend on the G or B string for cleaner tone.

🏎️

Slides

Glissando between notes — slide your finger along the fretboard without picking again.

Fret Slide String Skip Pre-bend
Slide example: e|---8---10\12---10---| B|--------------------| \ = slide down / = slide up

🎯 Tip: Keep your finger firm on the string while sliding. Land cleanly on target fret.

〰️

Vibrato

Rock your hand back and forth on a bent note to create a singing, expressive pitch variation.

Wide Vibrato Narrow Vibrato
B.B. King style vibrato: e|---8---8~---8-------| B|---------------------| ~ = vibrato (rock the string)

🎯 Tip: B.B. King said "It don't mean a thing if you can't intone." Vibrato is your signature.

🔨

Hammer-Ons

Strike a note by "hammering" your finger onto the fretboard — no pick needed for the second note.

Basic Double
Hammer-on example: e|---8h10---10h12---10---| B|-----------------------| h = hammer-on

🎯 Tip: Hammer from a lower fret. The harder you hammer, the clearer the note.

💨

Pull-Offs

Release a note by pulling your finger off the string, sounding the lower note underneath.

Basic Triple
Pull-off example: e|---10p8---8p0---8---| B|---------------------| p = pull-off

🎯 Tip: Don't just lift — pull slightly downward to "pluck" the string with your finger.

🤫

Palm Muting

Rest your picking hand's palm on the strings near the bridge for a tight, percussive sound.

Rhythm Choke
Palm-muted riff: e|---------------------| B|---------------------| G|---------------------| D|---5p0-x---5p0-x----| A|---5---x---5---x-----| E|---5-------5--------| x = percussive mute

🎯 Tip: Adjust palm position for more or less mute. Closer to bridge = tighter.

Practice Guide

A structured learning path from beginner to confident blues player.

📈 Your Learning Path

Week 1-2: Open chords & basic strumming Start here

Learn A7, D7, E7 open chords. Strum along to a slow 12-bar blues.

Week 3-4: Minor pentatonic position 1 Next

Learn the A minor pentatonic shape at 5th fret. Play single notes slowly.

Week 5-6: Bends & vibrato Locked

Add expression with bends and vibrato. Practice intonation.

Week 7-8: All 5 pentatonic positions Locked

Connect all 5 positions across the neck for full freedom.

Week 9-12: Licks, riffs & improvisation Locked

Combine everything into your own blues solos.

🎯 Essential Tips

  • Always use a metronome — start at 60 BPM
  • Practice slowly — speed comes with time
  • Record yourself to hear intonation issues
  • Play along with backing tracks on YouTube
  • Learn by ear, not just tabs
  • Keep a practice journal
  • Rest your hands — avoid overuse injury

🎛️ Recommended Gear for Blues

🎸 Guitar

Fender Stratocaster, Telecaster, or Gibson ES-335. Any electric guitar works — start with what you have!

🔊 Amp

Tube amp for warmth (Fender Deluxe, Vox AC30). Solid-state or modeling amps work great for practice.

🎛️ Effects

Overdrive (Tube Screamer), Wah pedal, Compressor. Keep it simple — tone is in your hands!
